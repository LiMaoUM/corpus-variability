> The question has an exact form once a corpus is a set of points. Embed each document, choose a distance $r$ that says what counts as the same content, and ask what share of the corpus lies farther than $r$ from every sampled document. We call that share the missing mass at resolution $r$. In the discrete case, where two documents either share a category or do not, it is the quantity Good and Turing estimated from the share of draws whose category was seen once \citep{good1953}. Ecologists built a sampling theory on that estimate: a rarefaction curve gives the coverage a smaller sample would have had, an extrapolation curve gives the coverage a larger sample will have, and together they say how many more units a target coverage needs \citep{chao2012coverage}. \citet{maurer2022missing} carried the estimator into metric spaces. His statistic $G$ is the share of sampled points with no other sampled point within $r$, and he proved bounds on its bias and variance. The estimator has had no application to text, and Maurer's treatment says nothing about how $G$ falls as the sample grows.
