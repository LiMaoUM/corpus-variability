## Text being read

> If we read 15 documents from a corpus, how much of the corpus have we seen? A survey sample comes with a sample size formula and a margin of error, and a sample of text comes with neither. We answer the question for one measure: the share of a corpus that has no document with the same content in the sample, where the same content means two embeddings closer than a distance $r$. A classical estimator due to Good, Turing and Maurer computes this share from the sample alone, as the share of sampled documents with no close match among the others. We show that on many corpora this estimator depends on the sample size $n$ and the distance $r$ only through $n r^{d}$, where $d$ is the intrinsic dimension of the corpus. This law yields a sample size formula, and in 77 of 83 tests the sample actually needed lay between two thirds and one and a half times the formula's answer. A pilot of 2{,}000 documents shows whether the law holds. With $r$ calibrated by large language models, 15 random posts show nearly every topic of a corpus that repeats a few claims, while samples of personal stories, forum questions and Truth Social posts cover little beyond themselves. On two corpora the answer depends on which model judged the pairs.
