## Text being read

> A survey sample comes with a margin of error and a sample size formula, and a corpus sample with neither. We give analogues of both for the share of a corpus farther than a cosine distance $r$ from every sampled document. Its estimator $G$, computed without labels, is the share of sampled documents with no other within $r$ \citep{maurer2022missing}. The nearest neighbour law predicts that $G$ depends on sample size $n$ and $r$ only through $n r^{d}$, with $d$ the intrinsic dimension. On seventeen corpus and encoder pairs, a fit on 1{,}000 documents predicts $G$ at seven times that size with out of sample $R^2$ above 0.92 where the law holds. Its prescribed sample size falls within a factor of 1.5 of the empirical one in 77 of 83 cases, and the Chao and Jost extrapolation errs two to nine times more. The factor $1 - n/N$ corrects $G$ for a finite corpus, and a fit on a small pilot sample flags where the law fails. With $r$ calibrated to mean the same content, a pilot separates corpora that a few hundred posts cover from corpora that need over 40\% of themselves, with two corpora sensitive to the judging model.
