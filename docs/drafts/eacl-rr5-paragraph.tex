\paragraph{Heterogeneity.} Does probe coverage degrade as the corpus grows more heterogeneous? We measure heterogeneity with two label-free statistics on the same GTR embeddings \citep{li2026coverage}. The first is the intrinsic dimension of the embedded documents, estimated by TwoNN \citep{facco2017}: AskDocs 13.4, Trump 9.3, TIFU 17.7 (means over the three seeds). The second asks whether one dimension describes the whole corpus. The share of sampled documents with no other sampled document within cosine distance $r$ follows a single curve in $n r^{d}$, with $d$ that dimension, when it does (held-out $R^2$ 0.95 for AskDocs and TIFU) and departs from it when it does not (0.56 for Trump, whose 20 $k$-means clusters have local dimension from 2.0 to 16.2, slogans beside discussion). Probe coverage is flat in $n$ on all three, on the corpus of highest dimension (TIFU) and on the corpus that no single dimension describes (Trump). Within this range of heterogeneity, coverage does not degrade with $n$. A corpus broader than TIFU at the same $k$ would need more components, and Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} does not test that case.
