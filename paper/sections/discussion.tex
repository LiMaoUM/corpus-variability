\section{Discussion}

The procedure a user follows has four steps. Choose the resolution $r$ that matches the question: a coarse $r$ for ``are the main kinds of post represented'', a fine $r$ for ``has every specific claim been seen''. Embed a pilot of about 2{,}000 documents and estimate $d$ by TwoNN. Fit Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} on nested subsamples of the pilot and check its $R^2$. Take $n^{*}$ from Equation~\ref{eq:nstar} and, after the sample is drawn, report $G\,(1 - n/N)$ as the share of the corpus the sample leaves uncovered. No step needs labels, and no step needs a GPU once the embeddings exist.

The survey analogy holds in two places and is instructive in a third. The finite population correction is the same factor for the same reason. The dimension $d$ plays the role of the variance: it is the one property of the population that enters the sample size formula, and like the variance it is estimated from a pilot, with $a$ and $b$ fitted alongside it. The third place is the margin of error. A survey's margin bounds the error of a proportion, and a corpus sample's plain mean already does that job well, to within 0.01 for cluster proportions at $n = 1{,}000$ (Appendix~\ref{app:bound}). The missing mass bounds something else, the mass of content the sample has no example of, which is the error that matters for enumeration: listing the themes, finding the rare claim, checking that a summary had the chance to mention something. A report of a corpus sample should carry both.

The theme result explains two things an applied reader will have met. A free summary written from a sample is stable across draws at a rate set by the summarizer's own granularity (Appendix~\ref{app:summaries}), and a theme inventory of fixed size is covered at the same rate in every corpus. Both measures are relative to the corpus, and neither can see how broad the corpus is at any absolute scale. The breadth that the sample size formula is about lives at a resolution, and only a measure at that resolution can see it.

The collapse diagnostic has a use beyond the formula. A corpus whose isolation surface does not collapse holds material at more than one scale, slogans and discussion in the Trump corpus, and a single sample size will oversample one and undersample the other. The probe of local dimension across clusters locates the parts, and a stratified sample with a size per part is the next step, which we have not tested.
