\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

We test the law on two kinds of data. Synthetic data have a known answer, so they show whether the method recovers the truth. Real corpora show whether the law holds on text that researchers actually sample. Table~\ref{tab:collapse} lists all of them.

The synthetic data come in two families. The Gaussian manifolds place 20{,}000 points on a random subspace of dimension $d \in \{2, 4, 8\}$ inside a space of 64 dimensions, so the true intrinsic dimension is known. The Zipf mixture draws 20{,}000 points around 200 topic centres, with topic sizes that follow a Zipf distribution (a few large topics and many small ones), adds Gaussian noise, and scales each point to length 1, so the topic of every point is known.

The ten real corpora come from four sources and cover several kinds of text. Three forum corpora from Reddit were assembled for earlier work on summarizing corpora. AskDocs holds medical questions, Trump holds posts about the 2020 presidential election, and TIFU holds personal stories told in the first person. Each has 10{,}000 documents after cleaning and the removal of duplicates. We embed them twice, with GTR-T5-base \citep{ni2022gtr} on three random samples of the source (seeds 42 to 44), and with Qwen3-Embedding-8B \citep{qwen3embedding2025} on the first of these samples. In the tables, the three GTR samples of a forum corpus count as one pair of corpus and encoder, so the three forum corpora give six pairs. With the five Census slices, Truth Social, Bluesky and the four synthetic sets, this makes the seventeen pairs of Table~\ref{tab:collapse}.

Five corpora come from a 2020 collection of tweets about the United States Census. Each is a slice selected by a keyword (citizens, trump, covid, illegal) or, for the random slice, drawn without a keyword. After removing links and mentions and dropping tweets with identical text, the slices hold between 7{,}610 and 30{,}136 tweets.

Two corpora are random samples of 50{,}000 English posts each from Truth Social and Bluesky, written between May and December 2024. We collected the public posts through the platforms' public interfaces. For the samples we kept posts marked as English with at least 20 characters after links and mentions were removed, and we dropped posts with identical text, which also removes reposts that copy their original. The Truth Social collection was built from a keyword search for ``trump''. It holds the matching posts, the threads under them, and the timelines of many of their authors. The Bluesky collection was built from a search for election terms, such as the names of candidates and parties, ``election'' and ``politics'', and it also holds many posts that match none of these terms, added by a crawl whose rule for adding posts was not recorded. Neither collection is a sample of its platform.

All Census, Truth Social and Bluesky corpora are embedded with Qwen3-Embedding-8B at 4{,}096 dimensions, with text cut at 256 tokens and each embedding scaled to length 1. We use cosine distance throughout. Cosine distance is not a metric in the strict sense, but it orders neighbours exactly as the metric $\sqrt{2(1 - \cos)}$ does, so every nearest neighbour in this paper is the same under either.

The posts are public, and the analysis uses only their embeddings and statistics computed over whole corpora. No post or account is quoted or identified in the paper. We release the post identifiers of the Truth Social and Bluesky samples, the pair judgments and the code, and no post text\ifdefined\arxivversion{} (\url{https://github.com/LiMaoUM/how-many-documents})\fi.
