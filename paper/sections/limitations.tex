\section*{Limitations}

The resolution $r$ is a cosine distance in one encoder's space. The law held under both encoders on AskDocs and TIFU, but $d$ moved with the encoder (18.3 under GTR against 13.6 under Qwen3 on TIFU), so a user must fix the encoder before choosing $r$ and report the two together. Within one encoder the same $r$ also means different things in different corpora (Section~\ref{sec:rcal}), and the calibration rests on model judgments without a human check.

The required sample size grows as $r^{-d}$, so the formula is useful only at resolutions where $n^{*}$ is a feasible number. At $d = 15$, halving $r$ multiplies $n^{*}$ by about 33{,}000. The formula plans samples at the resolution of the question and cannot make a fine resolution affordable.

The constants $a$ and $b$ are fitted on the pilot. We have an account of why $b$ is below 1 but no expression that predicts it.

The pilot threshold of 0.95 was chosen on the corpora of this paper, although the agreement of the two dimension estimates separates the same pairs without it. The formula's error band of a factor of 1.5 is empirical, and resampling the pilot does not reproduce it. The corpora are English social media and forum text, and the Truth Social and Bluesky collections are political pools, so their $d$ describes those collections.

The test unit is the document. Posts by the same account are correlated, and a design that samples accounts would need a cluster version of the estimator.
