\section{Results}

\subsection{The estimator tracks true topic coverage}

The Zipf mixtures give every document a topic, so the true topic mass covered by a sample is known. Over four settings, three seeds and eleven sample sizes from 50 to 5{,}000, coverage $1 - G$ at a radius of half the median pairwise distance of a pilot tracks the true coverage with Spearman $\rho = 0.968$ and a mean absolute error of 0.029 (Appendix~\ref{app:smoke}). Good-Turing on the true labels, which $G$ does not see, reaches $\rho = 0.989$. At smaller radii the agreement falls away. At those radii $G$ counts a document as isolated even when its topic is in the sample.

\subsection{The isolation rate collapses on $n r^{d}$}
\label{sec:collapse}

Table~\ref{tab:collapse} reports the fit of Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} for 17 pairs of corpus and encoder. Ten pass the diagnostic: the three manifolds, AskDocs and TIFU under both encoders, the random census slice, Truth Social and Bluesky. On these ten, a fit on cells with $n \le 1{,}000$ explains 92\% or more of the variance of $\log(-\log G)$ on cells with $n$ up to 7{,}000. Fitting on $n \le 300$ instead, a 23-fold extrapolation, keeps it above 0.92. Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} shows the surfaces against $n r^{d}$.

The fit with $\log n$ and $\log r$ as separate regressors gives a second estimate of $d$ that does not use TwoNN. On the ten passing pairs the two estimates differ by at most 19\%. On the seven failing pairs they differ by 27\% to 43\%, and by 46\% on one of the three GTR samples of Trump, with the regression estimate always lower.

Two baselines predict $G$ at the largest $n$ from the cells with $n \le 1{,}000$. A separate curve in $n$ at each radius, which needs no $d$, errs 1.5 to 3.8 times more than the collapse law on nine of the ten passing pairs, and less on the random census slice (0.025 against 0.040). The Chao and Jost extrapolation \citep{chao2012coverage}, with singletons and doubletons taken as sampled documents that have no other sampled document within $r$ and exactly one, run within its recommended range of a doubling from $n = 1{,}000$ to 2{,}000, errs 2 to 9 times more than the collapse law on the passing pairs. Both baselines beat the collapse law on the Zipf mixture, which fails the diagnostic.

\subsection{The sample size formula}

For every pair, Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} was fitted on cells with $n \le 1{,}000$, and Equation~\ref{eq:nstar} gave $n^{*}$ for targets $\varepsilon \in \{0.3, 0.2, 0.1\}$ at every radius where the mean surface crosses $\varepsilon$ beyond $n = 1{,}000$. The prediction was compared with the empirical crossing. On the ten passing pairs, 77 of 83 predictions fall within a factor of 1.5 of the empirical sample size. On the seven failing pairs, 10 of 23 do, and 10 of 18 on the six failing text corpora (Appendix~\ref{app:nstar}). Fitting $a$ and $b$ inside one 2{,}000-document pilot, as a user would, gives 76 of 83, with $d$ the mean TwoNN estimate over five pilots.

The error of $n^{*}$ comes from the shape of the law more than from the pilot. Resampling the pilot 50 times and refitting $d$, $a$ and $b$ each time gives intervals with a median width of 19\% of $n^{*}$, and these cover the empirical crossing in only 21\% of the 81 passing cases (a fresh set of draws puts two of the 83 crossings below $n = 1{,}000$). We therefore report $n^{*}$ with the empirical band of a factor of 1.5, which held in 93\% of cases on passing corpora and in at least three of five on each (Bluesky).


\subsection{The diagnostic}

The diagnostic must work before the sample is drawn, so we compute it inside one pilot of 2{,}000 documents: fit Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} on subsamples of size up to 750, and score $R^2$ on subsamples of size 1{,}000 to 2{,}000. The ten passing pairs reach a pilot $R^2$ of 0.96 or more, and the seven failing pairs reach at most 0.93 (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). A pilot threshold of 0.95 therefore reproduces the full diagnostic on every pair. The threshold was chosen on these corpora, and the agreement of the two dimension estimates gives a second check: the two differ by at most 19\% on every passing pair and by more than a quarter on every failing pair.

Section~3.2 says when the law should fail: when the effective dimension changes with the radius. On Trump the probes show such a change (Table~\ref{tab:probe} in Appendix~\ref{app:probe}). Clusters of local dimension near 2 hold slogans and clusters near 16 hold discussion, so different clusters dominate the count of isolated documents at small and at large radii. Removing the 3.4\% of documents with a near duplicate leaves the failure in place. The probes do not separate the failing census slices from the passing corpora.

\subsection{The finite population correction}
\label{sec:fpc}

Table~\ref{tab:fpc} in Appendix~\ref{app:fpc} compares $G$ and $G\,(1 - n/N)$ with the true missing mass $M$ of the finite corpus on the eight forum and census corpora under Qwen3, at sampling fractions from 0.01 to 0.7 and three radii, 144 cells. Uncorrected, $G$ has a mean absolute error of 0.114 and a maximum of 0.49. Corrected, the mean error is 0.012 and the maximum 0.11, and at $n/N = 0.5$ the error is 0.004. The correction holds on the corpora that fail the collapse.

\subsection{Choosing the resolution}
\label{sec:rcal}

The resolution $r$ is a cosine distance, and a user needs to know what content it groups. For each of the ten real corpora under Qwen3, we drew about 40 document pairs in each of ten distance bins from 0 to 1, 3{,}913 pairs in all, and asked Qwen3.8-27B whether the two documents are about the same specific content (the same claim, question, event or story), the same general topic, or unrelated (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). The share judged the same content falls with distance in every corpus. Where it crosses one half depends on the corpus, from $r \le 0.05$ on TIFU to 0.28 on a census slice, and on Bluesky it stays below one half even for the nearest pairs (Table~\ref{tab:rcal}). A cosine distance therefore carries no fixed meaning across corpora.

A second model, Gemma-4-31B-it, judged the same pairs with the same prompt. The two agree on 71\% of pairs (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.55$; $\kappa = 0.68$ for same content against the other two labels). Gemma calls more pairs the same content, 39\% against 28\%, so its crossing lies farther out in every corpus, by 0.02 to 0.16. On six of the nine corpora the 90\% intervals of the two crossings, from resampling pairs within bins, do not overlap (Table~\ref{tab:rcal}). The choice of judge therefore moves $r_{\mathrm{same}}$ more than the sampling of pairs does, and the judge is part of the definition of $r$, to be reported with the encoder.

\subsection{What a target costs}
\label{sec:feasible}

Table~\ref{tab:rcal} gives, under each judge, the random sample after which half of the corpus lies within $r_{\mathrm{same}}$ of a sampled document. We measure it on the whole corpus, where $G(n, r_{\mathrm{same}})\,(1 - n/N)$ falls to one half, from three draws at each $n$ up to $0.7N$. We also predict it from the pilot alone. The law fitted inside one pilot (Section~5.3) gives $G(n, r_{\mathrm{same}})$ at every $n$, and the same correction gives the crossing. The eighteen pairs of corpus and judge test the prediction unequally. Five cross below the smallest sample, and the prediction puts all five below 100 documents. Four (AskDocs and TIFU) sit at the cap with $G$ at 1.00, which any law that keeps $G$ near 1 predicts. Five cross inside the range of $n$ the pilot fit used, and the prediction is within a factor of 1.31. Four extrapolate. On census covid under Qwen3.8 the prediction is 10\% high. On Truth Social under both judges and on census random under Qwen3.8, the measured share lies near the cap, at 41\% to 44\%. The prediction is 3.6 to 3.8 points higher, so it recovers only about half of the distance below 50\%. It overstates the share on all twelve rows of these two corpora, and the largest errors fall at the ends of the interval of $r_{\mathrm{same}}$: 4.4 points on census random, and a factor of 1.83 on census covid under Gemma. The pilot thus puts each corpus in the right group, and near the cap it errs toward a larger sample.

The calibration can also stay inside the pilot. Gemma judged about 400 pairs drawn within the first pilot of census random, Truth Social and the Trump forum. The 90\% intervals of their crossings contain the values from pairs over the whole corpus, and with the pilot's fit they predict the share within 2.4 points of the measured one (Table~\ref{tab:pilotcal} in Appendix~\ref{app:pilotcal}). The whole procedure thus runs on the pilot.

The corpora fall into two groups. On the Trump forum corpus and the citizens, trump and illegal census slices, whose posts repeat a few claims, at most 604 documents cover half of the corpus under either judge. On AskDocs, TIFU and Truth Social the sample must hold 41\% to 50\% of the corpus, close to the 50\% a corpus needs when no two of its documents share content. A pilot of 100 documents tells the groups apart: $G$ is 1.00 on AskDocs, TIFU and Truth Social and at most 0.90 on the repetitive corpora. These corpora fail the diagnostic, and their crossings lie inside or below the range of $n$ the pilot fit used, so the prediction there interpolates. The diagnostic governs extrapolation in $n$ and transfer of the fit across $r$, which is what Equation~\ref{eq:nstar} needs. Two corpora depend on the judge. Census covid needs 19\% under Qwen3.8 and 1.6\% under Gemma, and census random, the slice drawn with no keyword filter, needs 44\% and 7\%. Across the 90\% interval of $r_{\mathrm{same}}$ the groups hold for the other seven corpora, except that census trump reaches 18\% at the near end of the Qwen3.8 interval.
