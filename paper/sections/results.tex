\section{Results}

\subsection{The estimator tracks true topic coverage}

The Zipf mixtures give every document a topic, so the true topic mass covered by a sample is known. Over four settings (50 to 500 topics, two noise levels), three seeds and eleven sample sizes from 50 to 5{,}000, coverage $1 - G$ at a radius of half the median pairwise distance of a pilot tracks the true coverage with Spearman $\rho = 0.968$ and a mean absolute error of 0.029 (132 sample points). Good-Turing on the true labels, which $G$ does not see, reaches $\rho = 0.989$ and error 0.016. At a tenth of the median distance the agreement is gone ($\rho = 0.31$). The radius matters, and the next result gives the dependence on it.

\subsection{The isolation rate collapses on $n r^{d}$}
\label{sec:collapse}

Table~\ref{tab:collapse} reports the fit of Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} with $d$ fixed at its TwoNN estimate. On the three manifolds the fit explains 95\% to 97\% of the variance of $\log(-\log G)$, against 4\% to 6\% for $\log n$ alone and 64\% to 73\% for $\log r$ alone. On AskDocs, TIFU, the random census tweets, Truth Social and Bluesky it explains 95\% to 99\%. Fitted on cells with $n \le 1{,}000$ and tested on cells with $n$ up to 7{,}000, the law keeps $R^2$ between 0.93 and 0.99 on these five corpora, and the $G$ it predicts at $n = 7{,}000$ has a mean absolute error of 0.044 on AskDocs, 0.029 on TIFU, 0.051 on the tweets, 0.039 on Truth Social and 0.018 on Bluesky. Across seeds 42 to 44 on the forum corpora the out of sample $R^2$ is 0.950 (sd 0.019) on AskDocs and 0.954 (sd 0.004) on TIFU. Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} shows the surfaces against $n r^{d}$.

The fit with $\log n$ and $\log r$ as separate regressors recovers the dimension without TwoNN. The ratio of the two coefficients is 2.1, 4.4 and 7.3 on the manifolds of true dimension 2, 4 and 8, and 15.4 and 19.2 on AskDocs and TIFU against TwoNN values of 13.6 and 17.7, within 15\% in every case. The exponent $b$ is below 1 on every corpus: 0.60 to 0.75 on the Gaussian manifolds, whose density falls off from the centre, 0.45 on AskDocs and TIFU, 0.46 on Truth Social, 0.38 on Bluesky and 0.23 on the tweets.

The sample size formula was tested directly. For each corpus, Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} was fitted on cells with $n \le 1{,}000$, Equation~\ref{eq:nstar} gave $n^{*}$ for targets $\varepsilon \in \{0.3, 0.2, 0.1\}$ at every radius where the surface crosses $\varepsilon$ beyond $n = 1{,}000$, and the prediction was compared with the crossing interpolated from the grid. On AskDocs, TIFU and the three manifolds, 20 of 24 predictions lie within a factor of 1.5 of the empirical sample size and all 24 within a factor of 2 (median ratios 0.93, 1.06, 1.21, 1.38 and 0.97). On Trump and the Zipf mixture, which fail the collapse, the predictions are off by factors of 3 and 80 (Appendix~\ref{app:nstar}).

\subsection{Where the collapse fails}

Trump and the Zipf mixture fail the fit (out of sample $R^2$ 0.64 for both, and 0.56 with sd 0.09 for Trump across seeds). Two probes locate the difference. In a 5{,}000-document sample of Trump, 3.4\% of documents have a near duplicate within cosine distance 0.02, against 0.0\% in AskDocs and TIFU and below 0.1\% on Truth Social and Bluesky. The TwoNN dimension of Trump's twenty $k$-means clusters runs from 2.0 to 16.2 (coefficient of variation 0.52), against 0.15 for AskDocs, 0.10 for TIFU, 0.16 for Truth Social and 0.13 for Bluesky. The clusters of dimension near 2 hold slogans and the clusters near 16 hold discussion. Removing the 266 documents of the full corpus that have a near duplicate leaves the out of sample $R^2$ at 0.74, so duplication accounts for part of the failure and the spread of local dimension is the remaining candidate. The Zipf mixture has no duplicates and its local dimension varies less (coefficient of variation 0.21), but its topic masses span two orders of magnitude, so the density around a document varies by the same factor. Both corpora have more than one scale, and one $d$ cannot describe them. The $R^2$ of the collapse fit is therefore a diagnostic: a corpus that collapses has one dimension and one sample size formula, and a corpus that does not fails the formula, as the tests of $n^{*}$ above show.

\subsection{The finite population correction}

Table~\ref{tab:fpc} compares $G$ and $G\,(1 - n/N)$ with the true missing mass $M$ of the finite corpus over four corpora (AskDocs, Trump, TIFU and the Zipf mixture), six sample sizes from 100 to 5{,}000 and six radii, 144 cells. Uncorrected, $G$ has a mean absolute error of 0.068 and a maximum of 0.42, reached at $n = 5{,}000$ of $N = 10{,}000$, where $G$ is twice $M$. Corrected, the mean error is 0.015, the maximum 0.11 and the Spearman correlation 0.996. Across the three seeds on the forum corpora the corrected error is 0.010 on AskDocs, 0.013 on TIFU and 0.009 on Trump, and on Truth Social and Bluesky, where $n/N$ is at most 0.1, it is 0.011 and 0.013. The correction holds on the corpus that fails the collapse as well.

\subsection{Theme coverage is a question about a partition}
\label{sec:themes}

With thirty themes per corpus, the share of theme mass present in a sample is the same in all eight forum and census corpora at every $n$: 0.58 to 0.65 at $n = 25$, 0.82 to 0.86 at $n = 50$, 0.94 to 0.98 at $n = 100$ and 0.99 to 1.00 at $n = 200$ (Figure~\ref{fig:themes}a). Equation~\ref{eq:gt}, evaluated on each corpus's theme masses, reproduces these values with a mean absolute error of 0.005. Over the same samples, coverage at a fixed resolution, $1 - G$ at $r = 0.34$ (the median nearest neighbour distance in 50-document samples pooled over the eight corpora), runs from 0.003 on AskDocs to 0.77 on the citizens tweets at $n = 25$ (Figure~\ref{fig:themes}b). The Spearman correlation across the eight corpora between the two quantities is $+0.12$ at $n = 50$ and $+0.07$ at $n = 200$.

The language model's enumeration behaves as the partition says it should. The model answers with the identifiers of inventory themes it finds present, each with a supporting document. Within each corpus the mass of themes it reports rises with $n$ along the label coverage curve, from 0.44 to 0.60 at $n = 25$ to 0.96 to 1.00 at $n = 400$, and the share of reported themes that the labels confirm rises from 0.73 at $n = 25$ to 0.98 at $n = 400$. Merging the eight inventories into one shared taxonomy, by clustering their centroids at four cosine thresholds (42 to 187 shared themes), gives corpora different shared coverage at the same $n$. Equation~\ref{eq:gt} on the shared masses predicts those differences (Spearman $+0.93$ to $+1.00$ across corpora at every $n$ and threshold), and $G$ at a fixed radius does not ($+0.31$, $+0.21$, $+0.38$ and $-0.40$ at $n = 50$ for the four thresholds). Appendix~\ref{app:shared} gives the full table.

The two questions are therefore different functionals of the same geometry. Coverage of a partition is set by the partition's mass vector. The missing mass at resolution $r$ is set by the local geometry at scale $r$. The two coincide when the partition's cells are balls of radius $r$, and thematic clusters are not. A user who asks ``have I seen every theme'' should use Equation~\ref{eq:gt} with the theme masses, which a pilot inventory supplies. A user who asks ``is every document within $r$ of something I have read'' should use $G$, the collapse law and Equation~\ref{eq:nstar}. The stability of a free summary written from a sample is a third measure, and Appendix~\ref{app:summaries} shows that it follows coverage within a corpus and does not order corpora.

\subsection{Truth Social and Bluesky}

The two platform samples, 50{,}000 posts each, obey the law. Truth Social has $d = 12.3$ and Bluesky $d = 15.8$. The collapse explains 97\% and 99\% of the variance, the out of sample $R^2$ is 0.93 and 0.99, and the predicted $G$ at $n = 7{,}000$ errs by 0.039 and 0.018. Fewer than 0.1\% of posts have a near duplicate and the local dimension across twenty clusters has a coefficient of variation of 0.16 and 0.13. At the same resolution, Bluesky's higher $d$ means that a target isolation rate needs more posts there than on Truth Social, by a factor of $r^{-3.5}$.
