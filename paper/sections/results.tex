\section{Results}

\subsection{The estimator tracks true topic coverage}

The Zipf mixtures give every document a topic, so the true topic mass covered by a sample is known. Over four settings (50 to 500 topics, two noise levels), three seeds and eleven sample sizes from 50 to 5{,}000, coverage $1 - G$ at a radius of half the median pairwise distance of a pilot tracks the true coverage with Spearman $\rho = 0.968$ and a mean absolute error of 0.029 (132 samples). Good-Turing on the true labels, which $G$ does not see, reaches $\rho = 0.989$ and error 0.016. At a tenth of the median distance the agreement is gone ($\rho = 0.31$). The radius matters, and the collapse law gives the dependence on it.

\subsection{The isolation rate collapses on $n r^{d}$}
\label{sec:collapse}

Table~\ref{tab:collapse} reports the fit of Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} with $d$ fixed at its TwoNN estimate. Eleven corpora pass the diagnostic: the three manifolds, AskDocs and TIFU under both encoders, the random census slice, Truth Social and Bluesky. On them the fit on cells with $n \le 1{,}000$ explains 92\% to 99\% of the variance of $\log(-\log G)$ on cells with $n$ up to 7{,}000, and the $G$ it predicts at the largest $n$ has a mean absolute error of 0.015 to 0.042. Fitting on $n \le 300$ instead, a 23-fold extrapolation, leaves the out of sample $R^2$ between 0.92 and 0.99. Across the three GTR samples of AskDocs and TIFU the out of sample $R^2$ varies by at most 0.012. Figure~\ref{fig:collapse} shows the surfaces against $n r^{d}$.

The fit with $\log n$ and $\log r$ as separate regressors recovers the dimension without TwoNN. The ratio of the two coefficients is 2.1, 4.4 and 7.3 on the manifolds of true dimension 2, 4 and 8. The exponent $b$ is below 1 on every corpus: 0.62 to 0.80 on the manifolds, 0.49 to 0.54 on AskDocs and TIFU, 0.43 on Truth Social and Bluesky, and 0.25 to 0.30 on the census slices.

The per radius Chao and Jost extrapolation is the ecological standard for the same task \citep{chao2012coverage}. From the singletons and doubletons at $n = 1{,}000$ at each radius it predicts $G$ at the largest $n$ with a mean absolute error of 0.10 to 0.22 on the corpora that pass the diagnostic, four to ten times the error of the collapse law. Its premise of a discrete species does not hold at a fixed radius, where a document's neighbourhood overlaps those of others.

\subsection{The sample size formula}

For every corpus, Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} was fitted on cells with $n \le 1{,}000$, Equation~\ref{eq:nstar} gave $n^{*}$ for targets $\varepsilon \in \{0.3, 0.2, 0.1\}$ at every radius where the mean surface crosses $\varepsilon$ beyond $n = 1{,}000$, and the prediction was compared with the crossing interpolated on the log scale between grid points. On the corpora that pass the diagnostic, 77 of 83 predictions fall within a factor of 1.5 of the empirical sample size and 81 of 83 within a factor of 2, with median ratios of 0.74 to 1.30 per corpus. On the corpora that fail it, 10 of 23 fall within 1.5 and 14 of 23 within 2, and the Zipf mixture is off by a factor of 70. Appendix~\ref{app:nstar} lists every corpus. The 90\% interval from resampling draws is narrow, a median of 7\% of $n^{*}$, and covers the empirical crossing in only 23 of 83 cases, so the error of the formula comes from the shape of the law and the interval reflects only sampling noise in the pilot.

A random sample can be compared with a sample chosen for coverage. Greedy farthest point selection, the core-set construction of \citet{sener2018coreset}, minimises the largest distance from any document to the chosen set. To bring the missing mass at a radius below a target, a random sample needs 0.6 to 1.0 times as many documents as greedy selection on the forum and census corpora (median 0.62 over 76 cases), and 0.12 times as many on Truth Social and Bluesky. Greedy selection spends its picks on outlying documents, which are few, while the missing mass weighs documents equally. For the share of the corpus left uncovered, random sampling is the better design, and it needs no embedding of the full corpus.

\subsection{Where the collapse fails}

Seven corpus and encoder pairs fail the diagnostic: the Trump forum corpus under both encoders, four of the five census slices, and the Zipf mixture. The diagnostic is conservative: on three of them (census covid and illegal, Trump under Qwen3) the formula still predicts within a factor of 1.5 in 7 of 8 cases, while on census citizens, Trump under GTR and the Zipf mixture it fails in 11 of 13. Two probes locate the difference on the GTR embeddings. In a 5{,}000-document sample of Trump, 3.4\% of documents have a near duplicate within cosine distance 0.02, against 0.0\% in AskDocs and TIFU and below 0.1\% on Truth Social and Bluesky. The TwoNN dimension of Trump's twenty $k$-means clusters runs from 2.0 to 16.2 (coefficient of variation 0.52), against 0.15 for AskDocs, 0.10 for TIFU, 0.16 for Truth Social and 0.13 for Bluesky. The clusters of dimension near 2 hold slogans and the clusters near 16 hold discussion. Removing the 266 documents that have a near duplicate raises the out of sample $R^2$ only to 0.74.

Section~3.2 says why a spread of local dimension breaks the law and a spread of density does not. A spread of density changes the shape of the curve in $n r^{d}$, which $b$ absorbs. A spread of dimension makes the effective $d$ change with the radius: at small $r$ the slogan clusters, where documents sit on a nearly flat set, dominate the isolation count, and at large $r$ the discussion clusters do. No single $d$ aligns the curves for different radii, which is the separation of colours visible for Trump in Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}. The Zipf mixture shows the same pattern for another reason. At small radii its documents look like noise around their centres, with the dimension of the noise, and at large radii they look like 200 points, the centres.

The failing census slices are those selected by a keyword that recurs in slogans and calls to action (citizens, trump, covid, illegal). The random slice, which was not selected on a keyword, passes.

\subsection{The finite population correction}

Table~\ref{tab:fpc} compares $G$ and $G\,(1 - n/N)$ with the true missing mass $M$ of the finite corpus on the eight forum and census corpora under Qwen3, at sampling fractions from 0.01 to 0.7 and three radii, 144 cells. Uncorrected, $G$ has a mean absolute error of 0.114 and a maximum of 0.49. Corrected, the mean error is 0.012 and the maximum 0.11, and at $n/N = 0.5$ the error is 0.004. The same comparison on the three GTR forum corpora and the Zipf mixture, at $n$ from 100 to 5{,}000, gives 0.068 uncorrected and 0.015 corrected. The correction holds on the corpora that fail the collapse.
