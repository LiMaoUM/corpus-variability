\begin{abstract}
A sample drawn from a survey population comes with a margin of error and a formula for the sample size a target precision needs. A sample drawn from a corpus to annotate or to summarize comes with neither. We supply both for one question: what share of the corpus lies within a chosen cosine distance $r$ of some sampled document, in a fixed embedding space. The share that does not is the missing mass at resolution $r$. Its estimator $G$ needs no labels: it is the share of sampled documents with no other sampled document within $r$ \citep{maurer2022missing}. We show that $G$, as a function of sample size $n$ and resolution $r$, depends on them only through $n r^{d}$, where $d$ is the intrinsic dimension of the embedded corpus estimated from a pilot of 2{,}000 documents. The dimension $d$ is therefore the corpus property that sets the sample size, in the role the variance plays in a survey formula. On three forum corpora and two social media platforms a fit on samples of at most 1{,}000 documents predicts $G$ at 7{,}000 documents to a mean error of 0.02 to 0.05, and the sample size it prescribes for a target isolation rate lands within a factor of 1.5 of the empirical one in 20 of 24 tested cases. For a finite corpus of $N$ documents the missing mass is $G\,(1 - n/N)$, the finite population correction of survey sampling, to a mean error of 0.01 to 0.02. On one corpus of political posts the dependence on $n r^{d}$ fails because its clusters have local dimension from 2 to 16, so the fit's $R^2$ tells a user whether one number describes a corpus. A second question, how many documents are needed to see every theme, concerns a partition and is answered by the theme masses alone: for thirty themes the answer is the same curve in all eight corpora we tested. Ten real corpora from four sources, two encoders and three seeds support these results.
\end{abstract}
