\begin{abstract}
A sample drawn from a survey population comes with two numbers: how far its estimates may sit from the population's, and how many more units a target precision needs. A sample drawn from a corpus to annotate or to summarize comes with neither. We supply both for one question, how much of the corpus lies within a chosen semantic distance $r$ of some sampled document. The share that does not, the missing mass at resolution $r$, has an estimator $G$ that needs no labels and that counts the sampled documents with no sampled neighbour within $r$ \citep{maurer2022missing}. We show that $G$ as a function of sample size $n$ and resolution $r$ collapses onto one curve in $n r^{d}$, where $d$ is the intrinsic dimension of the embedded corpus estimated from a 2{,}000-document pilot, so $d$ is the corpus property that sets the sample size, in the role the variance plays in a survey formula. A fit on samples of at most 1{,}000 documents predicts the isolation rate at 7{,}000 documents to a mean error of 0.03 to 0.05 on three real corpora. For a finite corpus of $N$ documents the missing mass is $G\,(1 - n/N)$, the finite population correction of survey sampling, to a mean error of 0.01. The collapse fails on a corpus whose local dimension runs from 2 to 16 across its clusters, so the fit's $R^2$ diagnoses whether one number describes a corpus. A second question, how many documents are needed to see every theme of a corpus, is a question about a partition, and its answer follows from the theme masses alone: for thirty themes it is the same curve in every one of eight corpora. Ten corpora from three platforms, two encoders and three seeds support these results.
\end{abstract}
