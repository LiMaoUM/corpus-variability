\section{The sample size formula by corpus}
\label{app:nstar}

For each pair of corpus and encoder, we fitted Equation~\ref{eq:collapse} on samples of up to 1{,}000 documents, with five independent samples at each size. Equation~\ref{eq:nstar} then gave the sample size $n^{*}$ that brings the isolation rate down to 30\%, 20\% or 10\%, at every resolution where the average over the five samples reaches the target only beyond 1{,}000 documents. We found the true sample size by interpolating between the sample sizes we drew, on a log scale. Table~\ref{tab:nstar} reports, for each pair, how many cases there were, the typical ratio of the formula's answer to the true sample size, and in how many cases the true sample size lay between two thirds and one and a half times the formula's answer (column $\le 1.5$) or between half and twice the answer (column $\le 2$). Two facts help in judging these numbers. Neighboring sample sizes in our grid differ by about one and a half times, and the true sample size varies by a median of 6\% across the five samples. On the six failing pairs of real text, which leave out the Zipf mixture, the true sample size lay within the narrower band in 10 of 18 cases and within the wider band in 14. When $a$ and $b$ are fitted on subsamples of one pilot of 2{,}000 documents instead of on draws from the whole corpus, the narrower band holds in 76 of the 83 cases on passing pairs. The wider band holds in 81 of them.

A random sample can be compared with a sample chosen for coverage. Greedy farthest point selection, the core-set construction of \citet{sener2018coreset}, minimises the largest distance from any document to the chosen set. To bring the missing mass at a radius below a target, a random sample needs 0.6 to 1.0 times as many documents as greedy selection on the forum and census corpora, and 0.12 times as many on Truth Social and Bluesky. Greedy selection spends its picks on outlying documents, which are few, while the missing mass weighs documents equally.

\section{Prediction of the sample that covers half the corpus}
\label{app:halfcover}

The predictions in this appendix use $d$, $a$ and $b$ from the first pilot alone. Averaging $d$ over five pilots instead moves no predicted percentage by more than half a percentage point.

The eighteen pairs of corpus and judge test the prediction to very different degrees. On five pairs the target is reached before the smallest sample of 100 documents, and at the point estimate of $r_{\mathrm{same}}$ the prediction also falls below 100. On four pairs (AskDocs and TIFU under both judges) the target is reached at the 50\% limit, with $G$ at 1.00. Any law that keeps $G$ near 1 predicts these four, so they tell little about the law or about $r$. On five pairs the target is reached between 100 and 1{,}000 documents, inside the range of sample sizes the pilot fit used. There the predicted sample is at most a third larger or a quarter smaller than the measured one.

The remaining four pairs require the law to predict beyond the pilot, and they are the real test. On the Census covid slice under Qwen3.8, the prediction is 8\% above the measured 3{,}356 documents. This corpus fails the pilot test, but the test checks the fit across many resolutions at once, and at a single resolution the fitted curve can still follow $G$. On Truth Social under both judges and on the random Census slice under Qwen3.8, the measured percentage lies near the 50\% limit, between 41\% and 44\%. The prediction is 3.6 to 3.7 percentage points higher. Measured from the limit, the corpus stands 6 to 9 points below 50\%, and the law places it only about half as far below.

The prediction is too high on all twelve rows of these two corpora, including the ends of the interval of $r_{\mathrm{same}}$. Where both a prediction and a measurement exist, the largest errors fall at those ends. On the random Census slice the prediction is 4.5 percentage points too high, and on the Census covid slice under Gemma the law predicts 197 documents where 106 suffice. At two ends where the measurement falls below 100 documents (the Census citizens and illegal slices under Qwen3.8), the prediction is about 150 documents. Near the 50\% limit the error always points toward a larger sample than needed. On the random Census slice, the full procedure of Appendix~\ref{app:pilotcal}, with $r_{\mathrm{same}}$ also found inside the pilot, predicts 721 documents where 532 suffice.

\section{Coverage of a theme inventory}
\label{app:themes}

Each of the eight forum and census corpora received inventories of themes by $k$-means on the full Qwen3 embeddings at $K = 10$, 30 and 100, and two inventories with skewed masses at $K = 30$: one from $k$-means++ seeding without refinement, one by merging the 100 clusters into 30 groups of Zipf sizes. The effective number of themes (the exponential of the entropy of the masses) ranges from 6.8 to 91. For each inventory and the 1{,}200 samples of Section~\ref{app:summaries}, Table~\ref{tab:themes} gives the range across corpora of the percentage of theme mass present. The expected coverage $\sum_k m_k (1 - (1 - m_k)^{n})$ matches the observed value to a mean absolute error of 0.004 over all cells. For the $K = 30$ inventory, Gemma~4 \citep{gemma4} was shown each sample and the list of named themes and asked which were present, with a supporting document for each. The mass of themes it reported rose with $n$ along the label coverage curve in every corpus, and the percentage of its reports that the labels confirm rose from 0.73 at $n = 25$ to 0.98 at $n = 400$.

\section{Summary stability}
\label{app:summaries}

For each of the eight forum and census corpora and each $n \in \{25, 50, 100, 200, 400\}$, thirty independent samples were summarized by Gemma~4 at temperature 0 in one paragraph of at most 150 words, and the summaries were embedded with Qwen3-Embedding-8B. Instability is the mean pairwise cosine distance among the thirty summaries. Within every corpus instability falls with $n$ and tracks $G$ (Spearman $+0.40$ to $+1.00$, seven of eight at $+0.70$ or above). Across corpora at a fixed $n$, Spearman of instability with $G$ is $+0.14$, $+0.12$, $+0.12$, $+0.07$ and $-0.17$ at $n = 25$ to 400, with bootstrap bands that include zero. TIFU illustrates why: its $G$ is near 1 at the shared radius and its summaries are the most stable, because the model summarizes it as stories of mishap whichever thirty stories it is given.

\section{A bound for functions that are stable at scale $r$}
\label{app:bound}

For a document function $f$ with range 1 that takes the same value on documents within $r$ of each other, the estimate of the corpus mean of $f$ formed by giving each corpus document the value of its nearest sampled document errs by at most $v(r) + M(r)$, where $v(r)$ is the proportion of covered documents whose nearest sampled document disagrees with them on $f$. On fifty cluster indicator functions over four corpora, six sample sizes and six radii, the bound held in 144 of 144 cells and was loose for proportions (median 22 times the realised error), where the plain sample mean already errs by 0.003 to 0.07.

\section{Prompts}
\label{app:prompts}

The large language models ran on NVIDIA H100 GPUs, served with vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm} at temperature 0. Nearest neighbor search and the fits used NumPy and scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011sklearn} on CPU.

\paragraph{Summary.} ``Below are $\{n\}$ documents sampled from a larger collection. Write a summary of the collection in one paragraph of at most 150 words. Cover the main themes in proportion to how often they appear, name notable minority themes briefly, and do not mention individual document numbers.''

\paragraph{Theme naming.} ``The $\{m\}$ documents below were grouped together because they are about the same theme. Give the theme a short name (at most 8 words) and a one-sentence definition that would let a reader decide whether a new document belongs to it. Answer as JSON with keys name and definition.''

\paragraph{Enumeration.} ``Below are $\{n\}$ documents sampled from a larger collection, followed by a list of $\{K\}$ themes found in the full collection. Decide which themes are present among these $\{n\}$ documents. A theme is present only if you can point to one document that is primarily about it; in a small sample most themes will be absent, and similar-sounding themes are distinct, so pick the single best theme for each document. Answer with one line per present theme in the form theme\_id: document\_number.''

\paragraph{Same content.} ``Two documents from the same collection are shown. Judge their relation. SAME: they are about the same specific content (the same claim, question, event or story), so a reader who has read one would learn little new from the other. TOPIC: they are about the same general topic but different specific content. UNRELATED: they are about different topics.''
