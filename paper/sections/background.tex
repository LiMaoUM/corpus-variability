\section{Background}

\subsection{Sample coverage in the discrete case}

A sample of $n$ draws from a population of categories covers the categories it has seen. The mass it has missed is the total probability of the unseen categories, and \citet{good1953} showed that the share of draws whose category appeared exactly once, $f_1/n$, estimates it with small bias. Ecologists took the estimator as the basis of a sampling theory for species. \citet{chao1984} bounded the number of unseen species from the singletons and doubletons $f_1$ and $f_2$, and \citet{chao2012coverage} wrote the expected coverage of a sample enlarged by $m$ draws as a closed form in $n$, $f_1$ and $f_2$, so that a survey of a habitat can be stopped at a target coverage instead of a target size. \citet{efron1976} asked how many words Shakespeare knew with the same machinery, and \citet{orlitsky2016unseen} gave the optimal prediction of the number of unseen species up to a sample about $n \log n$ times larger than the one in hand.

The estimators have reached text and machine learning a few times. \citet{hajic2025} apply the Chao bound to a catalogue of chant to say when a collection is close to complete. \citet{tran2017} fit species models to the answers of an open ended survey question to predict when new answers stop appearing. \citet{chung2018unknown} count unknown training examples with the same estimator, with a species defined as a unique example, and \citet{pal2026blindspot} estimate the mass of deployment states a classifier has never seen. In each of these a species is a discrete label, two items either match or do not, and the question of how many more items a target needs is answered only by \citet{hajic2025} and \citet{tran2017}.

\subsection{The missing mass in a metric space}

\citet{maurer2022missing} removed the discreteness. In a metric space the missing mass at scale $r$ of a sample $X_1, \dots, X_n$ is the probability that a new draw lies farther than $r$ from every $X_i$, and the estimator becomes
\begin{equation}
G(X, r) = \frac{1}{n}\,\big|\{k : d(X_k, X_i) > r \text{ for all } i \neq k\}\big| ,
\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
the share of sample points with no other sample point within $r$. In the discrete case, with $d(x, y) = 1$ for $x \neq y$ and $r < 1$, it is $f_1/n$. Maurer proved that $G$ overestimates the conditional missing mass by at most $1/n$ in expectation and that the variance of the difference is at most $3/n$ (his Theorem 2.1). The paper is theory: it sketches anomaly detection and coding as applications, and it contains no data, no embeddings and no statement about how $G$ falls with $n$. The nearest ecological construction is the attribute diversity of \citet{chao2019attribute}, in which two species closer than a threshold $\tau$ count as partly the same and diversity is reported as a profile in $\tau$. That profile is the analogue of our treatment of $r$ as a resolution the user chooses.

\subsection{Diversity of a sample in embedding space}

Several measures describe how spread out a set of embedded points is. The Vendi score is the exponential of the entropy of the kernel matrix's eigenvalues, an effective number of distinct items \citep{friedman2023vendi}, and its cousins extend it to the orders of a Hill number \citep{pasarkar2024cousins}. Precision and recall for generative models count points inside the nearest neighbour balls of a reference set \citep{kynkaanniemi2019}, density and coverage correct their bias \citep{naeem2020}, and MAUVE compares two distributions on a quantized embedding space \citep{pillutla2021mauve}. Each describes the sample in hand or compares two samples. None asks what a larger sample would cover, and none has a parameter in the role of a population variance.

\subsection{Saturation in practice}

Where a sample size for text is set at all, it is set by a stopping rule. Qualitative researchers stop interviewing when new interviews add no new code, and a review of 23 empirical tests puts that point at 9 to 17 interviews \citep{hennink2022, guest2006}. \citet{depaoli2024} carry the rule to coding by a language model and track the slope of the count of unique codes. TopicGPT reports that its topic count levels off after about 600 documents and stops when 200 documents add no topic \citep{pham2024topicgpt}. \citet{maeda2025} fit an model with eight topics to 522 open ended responses and find that 100 responses recover over 60\% of the full corpus's topics and 250 recover about 70\%. These are observations of a coverage curve. The present paper gives the curve a parameter and an estimator.
