## Editorial decision: Major Revision

Consensus across all five (every point below appears in at least three reviews):

1. **The n* test is miscounted and selectively reported.** The text says "20 of 24 within a factor of
   1.5"; Table 5 holds 38 cases for the named corpora, 32 within 1.5 and 38 within 2. The census
   random slice passes the collapse (out of sample R2 0.939) and fails n* (median ratio 2.13), and is
   excluded from "corpora that obey the law". Truth Social and Bluesky are never tested. The
   Devil's Advocate rates this CRITICAL.
2. **The theme result (5.5) follows from k = 30 k-means.** With near equal masses, Equation 6 gives
   1 - (29/30)^n = 0.57, 0.82, 0.97 at n = 25, 50, 100, which are the reported values. "The same
   curve in all eight corpora" describes the inventory procedure. The shared taxonomy test checks
   Equation 6 against its own labels. CRITICAL.
3. **No uncertainty anywhere.** One nested sample per grid cell, so R2 and the out of sample test
   are on dependent cells; no interval on G, d or n*; n* scales as r^(-d) and (1/b) reaches 4.3, so
   it is extremely sensitive to d, a and b. Maurer's 3/n variance bound is available and unused.
4. **The survey analogy overstates.** The FPC corrects an expectation here, a variance in surveys;
   d is not the only corpus parameter, a and b enter n* with large leverage; the "r^(-3.5)" factor
   between Bluesky and Truth Social ignores their different a and b.
5. **The collapse restates a known scaling.** Isolated vertices of a random geometric graph
   (Penrose 2003), the nearest neighbour distance law behind Levina and Bickel and TwoNN,
   Grassberger and Procaccia's correlation integral. The contribution is the application to G and
   its empirical test, and the paper should say so and cite them.
6. **Inconsistent numbers.** Census random d 14.5 (Table 2, BGE embedding, unstated) against 7.9 to
   13.0 (Table 1, Qwen3); AskDocs 13.4 vs 13.6 and Trump 9.3 vs 9.2 (seed mean vs seed 42, unstated);
   "three forum corpora" in the abstract where Trump fails; r = 0.34 in 5.5 against 0.30 in
   Appendix C; "+0.93 to +1.00" against table cells 0.81, 0.88, 0.90; Morris et al. 2023 cited as
   the source of the forum corpora (it is the vec2text paper).
7. **Encoder confounded with corpus.** GTR on the forums, Qwen3 elsewhere; the forum corpora were
   embedded with Qwen3 for the theme study but their collapse under Qwen3 is not reported.
8. **r and epsilon have no principled choice**, and the procedure is never used on a real decision;
   no baseline (per-r Chao and Jost extrapolation, k-center or core-set selection, pilot and
   extrapolate).

Disagreements: R1 (methodology) argues that density heterogeneity cannot explain the Zipf failure,
because averaging exp(-c n r^d) over c still collapses on n r^d; the likelier cause is a dimension
that changes with scale. The other reviewers accepted the paper's account. R1's point is correct as
mathematics and the revision must adopt it.

## The three changes most likely to move the decision (SAC)

1. Report the n* test on every corpus that passes the diagnostic, with seeds and intervals, and
   either make the diagnostic predict n* failure or state when the formula should not be used.
2. Run the collapse and n* tests on the same corpora under both encoders, and add a baseline that
   selects coverage directly (k-center or facility location) so readers see when random sampling
   plus n* is the right tool.
3. Reframe or drop the theme coverage claim (show it with unbalanced inventories, or present it as
   a caution about inventory-relative measures); present the finite population correction as
   bookkeeping; use the space for a principled choice of r.
