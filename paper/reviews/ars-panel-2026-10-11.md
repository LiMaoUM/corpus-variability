## Editorial decision: Major Revision

Consensus across all five (every point below appears in at least three reviews):

1. **The n* test is miscounted and selectively reported.** The text says "20 of 24 within a factor of
   1.5"; Table 5 holds 38 cases for the named corpora, 32 within 1.5 and 38 within 2. The census
   random slice passes the collapse (out of sample R2 0.939) and fails n* (median ratio 2.13), and is
   excluded from "corpora that obey the law". Truth Social and Bluesky are never tested. The
   Devil's Advocate rates this CRITICAL.
2. **The theme result (5.5) follows from k = 30 k-means.** With near equal masses, Equation 6 gives
   1 - (29/30)^n = 0.57, 0.82, 0.97 at n = 25, 50, 100, which are the reported values. "The same
   curve in all eight corpora" describes the inventory procedure. The shared taxonomy test checks
   Equation 6 against its own labels. CRITICAL.
3. **No uncertainty anywhere.** One nested sample per grid cell, so R2 and the out of sample test
   are on dependent cells; no interval on G, d or n*; n* scales as r^(-d) and (1/b) reaches 4.3, so
   it is extremely sensitive to d, a and b. Maurer's 3/n variance bound is available and unused.
4. **The survey analogy overstates.** The FPC corrects an expectation here, a variance in surveys;
   d is not the only corpus parameter, a and b enter n* with large leverage; the "r^(-3.5)" factor
   between Bluesky and Truth Social ignores their different a and b.
5. **The collapse restates a known scaling.** Isolated vertices of a random geometric graph
   (Penrose 2003), the nearest neighbour distance law behind Levina and Bickel and TwoNN,
   Grassberger and Procaccia's correlation integral. The contribution is the application to G and
   its empirical test, and the paper should say so and cite them.
6. **Inconsistent numbers.** Census random d 14.5 (Table 2, BGE embedding, unstated) against 7.9 to
   13.0 (Table 1, Qwen3); AskDocs 13.4 vs 13.6 and Trump 9.3 vs 9.2 (seed mean vs seed 42, unstated);
   "three forum corpora" in the abstract where Trump fails; r = 0.34 in 5.5 against 0.30 in
   Appendix C; "+0.93 to +1.00" against table cells 0.81, 0.88, 0.90; Morris et al. 2023 cited as
   the source of the forum corpora (it is the vec2text paper).
7. **Encoder confounded with corpus.** GTR on the forums, Qwen3 elsewhere; the forum corpora were
   embedded with Qwen3 for the theme study but their collapse under Qwen3 is not reported.
8. **r and epsilon have no principled choice**, and the procedure is never used on a real decision;
   no baseline (per-r Chao and Jost extrapolation, k-center or core-set selection, pilot and
   extrapolate).

Disagreements: R1 (methodology) argues that density heterogeneity cannot explain the Zipf failure,
because averaging exp(-c n r^d) over c still collapses on n r^d; the likelier cause is a dimension
that changes with scale. The other reviewers accepted the paper's account. R1's point is correct as
mathematics and the revision must adopt it.

## The three changes most likely to move the decision (SAC)

1. Report the n* test on every corpus that passes the diagnostic, with seeds and intervals, and
   either make the diagnostic predict n* failure or state when the formula should not be used.
2. Run the collapse and n* tests on the same corpora under both encoders, and add a baseline that
   selects coverage directly (k-center or facility location) so readers see when random sampling
   plus n* is the right tool.
3. Reframe or drop the theme coverage claim (show it with unbalanced inventories, or present it as
   a caution about inventory-relative measures); present the finite population correction as
   bookkeeping; use the space for a principled choice of r.

## Reviewer reports

The five reports are reproduced from the agent outputs without edits.

### SAC

Soundness 3, Excitement 3, ACL main 20%, Findings 45%. Reasons to accept: new application of a
known estimator with the missing scaling in n; validation of d without TwoNN; out of sample
prediction R2 0.93 to 0.99; FPC cuts error 0.068 to 0.015 and holds on Trump; honest failure
analysis. Reasons to reject: n* tested only where it works (census random excluded, TS and
Bluesky absent); theme result follows from the setup; FPC close to a definition; internal
inconsistencies (census random d, "three forum corpora", r^(-3.5)); encoder confounded with corpus.
Questions: why census random passes R2 and fails n*; why sample at random when k-center gives an
r-cover; how to choose r. Missing references: Sener and Savarese 2018; Levina and Bickel 2004;
Ansuini 2019; Pope 2021; Tulchinskii 2023; Gale and Sampson 1995; Chao et al. 2014; Abbas 2023.

### R1 methodology

Soundness 2.5, Excitement 3.5, ACL main 20%, Findings 50%. Reasons to reject: the derivation
contradicts the failure account (a Laplace transform in n r^d still collapses, so density
heterogeneity cannot explain Zipf; scale dependent dimension can); headline counts do not match
Table 5 (38 cases, 32 within 1.5); grid spacing of 1.53 makes "within 1.5" one grid step; single
nested sample per cell, no uncertainty, Maurer's 3/n bound unused, n* sensitivity to d, a, b; FPC is
a mean correction and only three corpora reach n/N = 0.5; theme result driven by balanced k-means
and "Good-Turing expectation" is a misnomer; Spearman over 8 corpora needs 0.74 for significance;
number disagreements across sections. Questions: is the regression ratio systematically above
TwoNN (15.4 vs 13.6, 19.2 vs 17.7); spread of n* across pilots; why TS and Bluesky are absent from
Appendix F. Missing references: Grassberger and Procaccia 1983; Levina and Bickel 2004; Penrose
2003; Evans, Jones and Schmidt 2002; Good and Toulmin 1956; Colwell et al. 2012; Chao et al. 2014;
McAllester and Schapire 2000; Baayen 2001; Sener and Savarese 2018; Pope 2021; Tulchinskii 2023.

### R2 domain

### R3 perspective

Soundness 3, Excitement 3, ACL main 20%, Findings 45%. Reasons to reject: n* evidence does not
match its table; survey analogy partly inaccurate (FPC scales variance in surveys, expectation

### Devil's Advocate

ACL main 10%, Findings 25% (40% after fixes). Strongest counter-argument: the three results restate
known identities or follow from the design. G is the isolated vertex fraction of a random geometric
graph and n r^d is its regime parameter (Penrose 2003); the collapse with TwoNN's d is a
consistency check between two readings of one nearest neighbour distribution, with three fitted
parameters per corpus for a monotone surface in two variables; FPC is bookkeeping; the theme curve
is the curve of 30 equal bins. CRITICAL: 20 of 24 vs 38 cases; "corpora that obey the law" chosen
after results; same curve follows from K = 30; shared taxonomy test confirms the framing by
construction. MAJOR: a and b hidden behind "d sets the sample size"; n* exponentially sensitive to
d (d = 15 plus or minus 2 moves n* by a factor of 11 at r = 0.3); R2 diagnostic built on one
failing corpus with no threshold and does not predict census random's n* failure; no extrapolation
baseline. Alternative explanations: curvature of cosine balls at large r for b < 1; heavy tailed
repetition for Trump; LLM enumeration sees the inventory. So what: a label-free G (1 - n/N) at a
chosen r is useful but is Maurer plus bookkeeping; no real decision shown; for d = 15 halving r
multiplies n* by 33,000, so the formula works only near saturation. Not defects: the coverage
framing, the FPC's empirical cleanliness, recovery of d on manifolds, separating partition from
resolution coverage, plain limitations.
